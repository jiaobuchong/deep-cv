\begin{abstract}
We adapt ViT-B/16 (pretrained on ImageNet-21k, fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k) to a fine-grained dataset with 10,000 classes and only 45 training images per class.
Each training run may use 2 hours on 48\,GB of GPU memory.
We compare seven ways to adapt the model under the same settings: a linear probe, fine-tuning the last few blocks, full fine-tuning, BitFit, Adapter, AdaptFormer and LoRA.
The choice that mattered most was how to start the new 10,000-class head.
The usual start, a linear probe trained on frozen features of the training images, made all three methods we tested worse.
Starting the head from the mean feature of each class (a prototype) raised holdout accuracy by 2.1--2.5 points for full fine-tuning, LoRA and AdaptFormer, and raised the validation accuracy of our final model by 3.1 points.
We also find that where the trainable parameters are matters more than how many there are: LoRA on 1.375\% of the backbone is as accurate as fine-tuning the last four blocks (33\%).
Training memory depends on how far back the gradients go, not on how many parameters are trained.
Our final full fine-tuning model, trained on all images and tested with horizontal flips, reaches 76.22\% top-1 on the CodaBench validation set (reference: 70.8\%) and 76.09\% on the test set, in 1.03 GPU-hours on one A100.
Our final LoRA model trains 1.375\% of the backbone and reaches 71.90\% on validation (reference LoRA: 68.3\%) and 71.67\% on test; after training it is merged into the weights, so it does not slow down inference.
We also report the ideas that did not work, and show after the deadline that larger input images add another 4.4 points on our holdout.
\end{abstract}
