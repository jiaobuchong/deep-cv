A practical lesson about cost. What PEFT clearly saves is trainable parameters and storage: 1.375% of the backbone, and a checkpoint of about 36 MB, most of which is the 10,000-class head, against 374 MB for full fine-tuning. What it did not save in our runs is training time or memory. Gradients still flow back through the whole frozen backbone, and the extra LoRA branches add work, so LoRA on all linear layers trained at 885 images per second against 1,131 for full fine-tuning, with slightly more peak memory. At inference, merging is what matters. LoRA merges into the weights and costs nothing extra. AdaptFormer cannot be merged and is about 35% slower at batch size 1.
