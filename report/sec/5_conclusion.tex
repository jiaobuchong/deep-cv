\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We adapted ViT-B/16 to a 10,000-class fine-grained benchmark with 45 images per class within a budget of 96\,GB$\cdot$h per run.
The largest gain came from the least glamorous component.
Initializing the 10,000-way head with imprinted class prototypes instead of a linear probe fitted on the training features improved accuracy by 2.1--3.1 points, for full fine-tuning, LoRA and AdaptFormer alike.
With this head, full fine-tuning reaches 76.22\% on the CodaBench validation set in 1.03 GPU-hours.
LoRA on all linear layers reaches 71.90\% while training 1.375\% of the backbone.
After merging, LoRA has zero inference overhead.
Across seven adaptation methods run with one protocol, three choices mattered for parameter-efficient adaptation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item where the trainable parameters go: small changes in every block beat fine-tuning whole top blocks with 12--69$\times$ fewer parameters, and LoRA works best on all linear layers, not only attention;
    \item whether the update can be merged (LoRA and BitFit \vs the two adapters);
    \item a short schedule (about 3 epochs).
\end{itemize}
Training memory, finally, follows how far gradients propagate rather than how many parameters are trained, so fine-tuning only the top blocks is the cheapest way to train, while spreading a few parameters over all blocks is the most accurate per parameter.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Every configuration was trained once (seed 0), and the holdout split has a noise level of about $\pm$0.3 points, so small differences such as the 0.26-point gain from the two extra images per class should be read with care.
The dataset is anonymized, so we cannot relate our findings to its domain.
One ablation batch ran on a different GPU; its accuracies are comparable, as checked by repeating one run, but its speeds and latencies are not.
Our explanation of why the linear-probe head hurts is consistent with the loss curves but is not a controlled proof.
The efficient methods share one learning rate that was not tuned per method, partial fine-tuning with $K = 4, 6$ uses a different recipe from $K = 1, 2$, and the Adapter and AdaptFormer also differ in scale, so these comparisons are not single-factor.
We did not explore prompt-based methods such as VPT~\cite{jia2022vpt} or prefix tuning~\cite{li2021prefix}, higher input resolution, or larger budgets.
